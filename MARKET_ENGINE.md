Market orders use available liquidity and cancel any unfilled remainder. Marketable limits fill only at their price or better, then rest. Players and automated participants now match in the same finite book; player executions change the tape, candles, and both counterparties' balances and positions. Resting orders use price priority followed by arrival priority. Changing a limit's price preserves its identity and protection but loses time priority; a no-op price amendment keeps it. Self trades across the same owner/profile cancel the older quote instead of generating volume. Cancelling one account's orders cannot affect another account.

Stops and profit targets are reduce-only, mutually exclusive closing triggers. They latch on each executed trade and cancel related working entries. Stop-loss, manual close, and liquidation consume real available liquidity and retry unfilled protective residuals on later scheduler steps. A triggered take-profit submits a limit at the target, retains its queue priority while resting, and cannot fill at a worse price. Neither kind guarantees a complete fill; stops can slip. Position entry is the weighted average of actual fills; adding in the same direction updates attached distances from that average. Closing cancels entries and retries a residual position without reversing it.

Checkpoint V9 persists the shared book and amendment priority. On migration from V1–V8, old off-book player limits are cancelled rather than retroactively matched; balances, positions, protective triggers, and existing tape are preserved. Runtime connection state is rebuilt from actual connected players before the exchange advances. The historical house ledger remains only for trusted administrative migration/reset settlement; ordinary player orders cannot use it.

`/bots stats` uses the same server-side owner check and sends a read-only snapshot only to the requesting owner. It includes totals, bot-type and market breakdowns, and individual bot rows, including retiring/close-only participants. Players, clearing accounts and archives are excluded. The UI does not claim to write the clipboard automatically. `Select report` reselects everything; the tab-separated tables can be pasted into a spreadsheet. Reports are bounded below Roblox's text-property size limit and failures are reported without exposing internal account data. `BotStatsTests` covers totals, exclusions, refresh baselines, deterministic/nonmutating inspection, owner/group authorization and failure handling.

The server-only `/bots refresh` command uses the same creator/group-owner authorization as `/reset` and `/give`. It accepts exactly `refresh`, queues one request, and starts before the next simulation request batch. The saved exchange must be available; local unsaved worlds cannot acknowledge a durable reset. Duplicate callbacks are debounced, overlapping operations are rejected, and a monotonically increasing checkpoint receipt prevents replay of the same operation.

Every active participant bot receives the existing `BotPopulation.Capital[Kind]` allocation as its new equity. Retiring/retired bots, close-only markets, players, clearing accounts and archives are excluded. The capital adjustment is allocation minus marked equity, so profitable bots may withdraw capital and negative-equity bots may require more than their allocation. No capital configuration, prices, candles, volume or execution records change.

Each bot records the adjustment, actor, sequence, simulation step, prior realized/unrealized P&L and cash change in `CapitalAdjustments`. `Base = StartingCapital + NetCapitalAdjustments` continues to mean contributed capital. `PnlBaseline` retains prior economic P&L; `Balance = Base + PnlBaseline + Realized`. Current reported P&L is `Realized + Unrealized`, also returned as `Summary.Session`. Open positions retain their strategy/execution `Entry` and gain an accounting-only `PnlEntry` at the refresh mark. Partial closes and scale-ins use that independent cost basis; reversals start a new basis. Consequently a close at the refresh mark earns zero even when the original entry differed. Trade history remains historical evidence, with new closing rows carrying the accounting entry when applicable.

`World:RefreshBots` prepares and audits detached accounting records, encodes the complete checkpoint, and waits for the fenced lease commit before applying those records to the original in-memory bot objects. Simulation is paused during the transaction. Bot strategies, original positions, pending exits, retry deadlines, order objects and priorities remain intact; player accounts and requests are not replaced by an epoch swap. A single eligibility revision wakes existing retry controls when newly funded resting liquidity becomes executable. Save ambiguity pauses and reloads the durable result instead of guessing or executing again.

Checkpoint V17 introduced these baselines and the receipt; older engines fail closed. Current checkpoints use V19. `BotRefreshTests` covers long/short open gains and losses, partial entry/exit fills, scale-ins/reversals, negative equity, queued protections, exclusion of retiring/closed-market bots, owner rejection, repeated commands, scheduler boundaries, storage failure and lost acknowledgments. Validation uses detached fixtures and captured-state copies, never an active account. ## Bot fair-value tracking and confirmed entries — 2026-10-18

Protective and Reversion bots now anchor each observation to current internal fair value with independent observation noise. They no longer treat a slowly updated historical price level as today's fair value. Reversion orders that add exposure require a deviation from current fair value and a price turn toward it over a three-second confirmation window. Reductions remain available without confirmation and are capped/reduce-only so they cannot accidentally reverse into an unconfirmed position.
